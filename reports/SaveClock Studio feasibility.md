# SaveClock Studio is feasible, but rebuild its core

SaveClock Studio is technically feasible and cheap to run, but the original blueprint (librosa, then Pexels/Pixabay by BPM/vibe, then MoviePy) needs three changes and carries one legal risk that engineering cannot remove. First, replace librosa with **Beat This!**, the only top-accuracy beat/downbeat tracker whose code and weights are both MIT-licensed; madmom's weights are non-commercial, essentia's code is AGPL and its models are non-commercial ([GitHub](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this), [madmom](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom), [Essentia](https://essentia.upf.edu/licensing_information.html)). Second, replace MoviePy with direct FFmpeg: for pure cut, concat and mux, nothing needs to touch pixels in Python, and a 30-second video then renders in roughly 20-60 s on 4 vCPU at well under a cent of compute (researchers' estimate, not benchmarked). Third, treat Pexels and Pixabay as per-job fetch sources, not a mirrored library, because both ban replicating their service or bulk copying ([Pexels docs](https://www.pexels.com/api/documentation/), [Pixabay terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)). A free-tier video costs about **$0.02-0.06** all-in (inference); a hybrid tier with a few AI-generated hero shots adds about **$0.40-0.80**; an all-AI video adds **$1.50-3.60** before retries. The unresolved risk is the user's uploaded song: the app reproduces and transforms copyrighted audio, and TikTok's general library licenses personal use only ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-library-explained)). The safest design exports with audio stripped plus a cut list, or muxes the audio only on a private render. Legal points in this report need a lawyer; nothing here is legal advice. Several sourced figures are aggregator-level or conflicting and are flagged inline.

## Beat tracking is near-solved for pop; weak spots are slow and irregular music

Beat This! (Foscarin et al., ISMIR 2024) matches or beats the prior state of the art in F1 without a DBN post-processor, but is weaker on continuity metrics ([arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658v1)). On pop and dance material it is very strong: in 8-fold cross-validation, beat/downbeat F1 is **97.5/95.3 on Ballroom, 95.8/90.7 on Harmonix, and 96.1/93.7 on RWC Pop** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658v1)). Because it trains on most of those sets, the more honest number is the test-only GTZAN result: **beat F1 89.1, CMLt 79.8, AMLt 89.8; downbeat F1 78.3** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658v1)). It degrades on difficult material: SMC beat F1 is 62.7, RWC Classical downbeat F1 66.3, ASAP downbeat 61.2. A 2026 SMC failure analysis found **8% of tracks (17/217) were octave (half/double-tempo) errors**, and that a DBN with a 55 BPM floor forces double-tempo on the 21% of SMC tracks slower than that ([arXiv 2605.12287](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12287)). For editing, an octave sanity check (fold tempo into roughly 70-180 BPM-equivalent) is therefore mandatory; this clamp is the researchers' recommendation, not a cited result.

Licensing is what separates the options. Beat This ships MIT code and MIT weights (final model about 78 MB, "small" about 8 MB with little loss, GTZAN beat F1 88.8), though its README warns that some training data is copyright- or CC-limited and tells users to assess whether that affects them ([GitHub](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this)). madmom's source is BSD but its model files are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, commercial use requires contacting Gerhard Widmer, and the PyPI release is from 2018 with reported Python 3.10+ and NumPy 1.24+ breakage ([GitHub](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/madmom/), [BeatNet](https://github.com/mjhydri/BeatNet)). Essentia's library is AGPLv3 for open use with a commercial license from UPF MTG, and its models are CC BY-NC-SA with a proprietary option on request; the models page and the licensing page disagree (NC-SA vs NC-ND), so the exact terms are unclear ([licensing](https://essentia.upf.edu/licensing_information.html), [models](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html)). allin1 (beats, downbeats, 10 structure labels) is MIT-labelled but depends on madmom, NATTEN and Demucs, and it has open issues asking about its license and commercial iOS use whose answers the researchers did not read ([GitHub](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one)). Runtime: no official Beat This figure exists; a third-party Rust port reports the Python reference took 5.1 s for a 4:32 file on an Apple M4 CPU ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/beat-this/1.0.0)). A 3-minute song on an x86 CPU worker at roughly 3-10 s is the researchers' extrapolation and needs measuring. No librosa `beat_track` benchmark on GTZAN, Ballroom or SMC was found; that librosa is clearly weaker than the deep trackers is expected but unquantified here.

## Structure, mood and clip matching: clean licenses exist only for parts

For choruses and sections, SongFormer (arXiv 2510.02797) beats All-In-One on SongFormBench-Harmonix, with boundary hit-rate at 0.5 s of **0.703 versus 0.596** and accuracy of 0.795-0.807 versus 0.740 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02797v2)). Its code is CC-BY-4.0, but the weights license is not stated and the MuQ/MusicFM backbones were not checked, so commercial use needs a legal check ([GitHub](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/SongFormer)). Classic chorus finders (loudest, most repeated) misdetect repeated verses ([DeepChorus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06338v2)). Because boundary precision is only about 60-70%, the researchers recommend snapping every section boundary to the nearest downbeat, which hides most errors; this is inference. The practical default is a cheap energy-envelope segmenter (RMS, onset density, spectral centroid) on the Beat This grid, with SongFormer or allin1 as an upgrade after licensing is cleared.

Mood and genre are the sharpest license trap. Essentia's mood, arousal/valence and genre models are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with a proprietary license on request, and MERT-v1-330M is CC BY-NC-4.0 ([Essentia models](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html), [HF](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/MERT-v1-330M)). LAION-CLAP's repo is CC0 and its music checkpoint reaches **71% zero-shot GTZAN**, enough for coarse vibe only, and its training data cannot be released for copyright reasons ([GitHub](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP)). The recommended path avoids NC weights: derive energy and valence from DSP features plus tempo, then let a small LLM turn them into stock-search keywords (under about $0.01 per video on Claude Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5 per million tokens, per a third-party price page that may predate newer models ([PricePerToken](https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/anthropic-claude-haiku-4.5))). On the visual side, SigLIP 2 is Apache-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/google/siglip2-base-patch16-224)) and can rank candidate clips against the keywords. TransNetV2 beats PySceneDetect at shot-boundary detection on ClipShots (F1 **0.726 versus 0.477**) and is useful to reject clips with internal cuts, though its original repo license was not confirmed ([Cosmos paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03575)). The one beat-aligned video-editing paper found (MVAA) regenerates footage with a diffusion model at about 10 minutes per video on an RTX 4090, so it does not suit stock cutting ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18881v1)). Cheap alignment of a clip's motion peak to a beat, using frame differencing, is the researchers' idea and has no cited accuracy.

Onset and drop detection was not researched with sources. Editing rules come from practitioner blogs, not studies: cut on beats, make bigger changes at four-bar phrase ends, avoid downbeat-only metronomic cuts, and use every-beat cuts only in short 4-8 beat builds ([RouteNote](https://licensing.routenote.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/9560), [Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/beat-based-editing-in-capcut-syncing-cuts-transitions-and-motion-to-music), [FilmDaft](https://filmdaft.com/?p=1340)). The proposed cut-rate table (low energy every 2-4 bars, mid every bar or 2 beats, drops every 1-2 beats, first cut per section on a downbeat, 20-40 ms early jitter) is a heuristic and unvalidated.

## Direct FFmpeg wins the render; a GPU is optional

MoviePy 2.x (latest found 2.2.1, May 2025) decodes frames into numpy and re-pipes them to FFmpeg, and v2 renamed much of its API (`moviepy.editor` removed, `.set_*` to `.with_*`) ([update guide](https://zulko.github.io/moviepy/getting_started/updating_to_v2.html)). No independent 2.x benchmark exists; one weak v1 test showed 155 s versus 142 s for software FFmpeg ([Dev.to](https://dev.to/johalputt/editing-software-for-youtube-what-no-one-tells-you-2jda)), and a rival library's claims (up to 4.6x, only 1.06x on plain re-encode) come from an interested party ([MovieLite](https://www.mintlify.com/francozanardi/movielite/resources/performance-benchmarks)). The case for FFmpeg is therefore architectural, not measured: a cut-and-mux job has no per-frame Python work to justify the round trip.

Frame accuracy is where the blueprint needs the most care, and the following FFmpeg guidance is the researchers' unsourced standard knowledge (the FFmpeg seeking wiki fetch was blocked). Stream copy can only cut on keyframes, and stock clips often carry multi-second GOPs, so cuts must re-encode. The recommended pipeline: normalize each fetched clip once to a mezzanine (for example 1080x1920, 30 fps, yuv420p, constant frame rate, common timescale), cut by integer frame index computed from absolute beat times as `f_i = round(t_i * fps)` (never by summing rounded durations), and use `trim=start_frame/end_frame` rather than `-t`. At 30 fps the worst-case error is ±16.7 ms per cut and does not accumulate. Mux the original song untouched against the video instead of concatenating audio. Either a single `filter_complex` trim+concat pass or per-clip intermediates joined by the concat demuxer works for 10-30 clips. Mezzanine caching is cheap: Cloudflare R2 is $0.015 per GB-month with free egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)), so about 1,000 cached 10-second all-intra clips (roughly 80 GB, the researchers' size estimate) cost about $1.2 per month.

GPU encoding barely changes the picture. Turing-or-newer NVENC is roughly x264 "medium" quality at high bitrates but falls behind at low bitrates ([goughlui](https://goughlui.com/?p=48157), [Igor's Lab](https://www.igorslab.de/en/nvidias-nvenc-vs-cpu-encoding-the-turing-video-encoder-for-twitch-streaming-co-comparison-analysis-with-netflix-vmaf/3/)). For a 900-frame output, the researchers estimate x264 veryfast at about 8-15 s of encode and a T4/L4 saving only a few seconds, because scale/crop is CPU-bound without CUDA filters; no L4/T4 single-stream figure was found, so benchmark before buying. Hosting options: Modal L4 at roughly $0.000222 per second (about $0.005-0.01 per video with overhead; aggregator prices, verify on modal.com) ([Modal](https://modal.com/blog/nvidia-l4-price-article)), or a CPU VPS. Hetzner raised cloud prices sharply on 15 June 2026 (dedicated CCX up roughly 113-176%, shared CPX up too) and secondary sources disagree on exact figures ([WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/hetzner-price-increase-june-2026-cpx-ccx-alternatives/), [Northflank](https://northflank.com/blog/hetzner-cloud-server-price-increases)). Serverless wins on idle cost; a standing box wins only at steady utilization. For queues, any process-based queue (Dramatiq, Celery, RQ) fits CPU-bound FFmpeg subprocesses better than async-native Arq, and Temporal is for durable multi-stage pipelines ([DEV guide](https://dev.to/datanestdigital/background-jobs-in-python-celery-vs-rq-vs-dramatiq-vs-arq-2026-decision-guide-37m6)). The researchers suggest idempotency keys (hash of timeline plus song), `-progress` parsing, SSE for progress, a 5-minute hard timeout and 1-2 retries; this is design inference. For instant preview, sequence proxy clips in the browser against the song's `<audio>` clock, and use a 270x480 proxy render of the same cut list as a verification step; neither was benchmarked.

Hosted render APIs are a prototype or overflow option. Shotstack's official pricing runs from $0.20 per minute pay-as-you-go to $0.05 per minute at volume ($39 per month for 250 minutes) ([Shotstack](https://shotstack.io/pricing/)); that is about $0.03-0.10 per 30 s video versus a fraction of a cent self-hosted. Creatomate, JSON2Video and Rendi figures come from competitor or review pages and conflict ([Wireflow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/creatomate-pricing), [Rendi](https://www.rendi.dev/pricing)). Remotion is free for individuals and companies of up to 3 employees, but its official pricing page did not render figures, and it adds React/Chromium overhead the job does not need ([license](https://remotion.dev/docs/license)).

## Free stock works for rendering but constrains caching, and audio is the real legal exposure

Pexels offers a free API (200 requests per hour, 20,000 per month by default; unlimited if terms are met) with `orientation=portrait` and vertical 1080x1920 files, and requires a prominent link back to Pexels ([API docs](https://www.pexels.com/api/documentation/)). Its license allows commercial use without attribution but bans selling unaltered copies and redistribution on other stock platforms ([license](https://www.pexels.com/license/)). Help-center material (read only via search summaries, one fetch returned 403) says compiling content into a similar service or copying Pexels' core functionality gets keys revoked ([Pexels help](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/900005851943-I-have-a-similar-marketplace-or-photo-library-to-Pexels-can-I-use-the-API-to-add-your-images)). The API terms page returned 404, so the exact storage rules are unknown. Pixabay allows 100 requests per 60 s, requires 24-hour response caching and downloading to your server, and offers no orientation filter on video; its license allows commercial use but forbids standalone resale and building a competing service ([API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/), [terms, updated 20 Oct 2026](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)). One earlier aggregator described Pixabay's redistribution rules differently, so verify the live text. Coverr's free API tier is for non-commercial use and requires its logo, so it is a poor fit for a paid product ([Coverr](https://api.coverr.co/docs)). Mixkit, Videvo, Openverse, Wikimedia, NASA and Internet Archive were not verified. The researchers read a render-per-job tool that transforms clips as a creative work consistent with these licenses, and a mirrored clip library as the risky pattern; nothing found confirms either reading, and whether commercially sold outputs are allowed is unaddressed. Identifiable people and logos in clips remain the app's and user's risk because neither license guarantees releases. Vertical-clip availability per keyword is unknown; the researchers recommend a 50-keyword portrait test on Pexels before committing.

The user-uploaded song is the larger exposure. DMCA §512 safe harbor needs a registered designated agent, a reasonably implemented repeat-infringer policy, and takedown handling; BMG v. Cox lost safe harbor for failing the policy ([summary](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/11/dmca-designated-agent-safe-harbor-takedown-repeat-infringer-guide); secondary source, statute not read). Safe harbor is built for storage at a user's direction and may not cleanly cover a transformation service (researchers' inference). In the EU, Article 17 targets services hosting and publicly sharing large amounts of works (CJEU C-401/19 upheld it, [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2022/04/breaking-article-17-of-dsm-directive-is.html)), a private render-and-return tool probably falls outside, and the DSA notice-and-action regime applies to hosts (sources are proposal-era commentary). Adding public share links makes platform-style obligations much more likely. Downstream, TikTok licenses its general library for personal, non-commercial use, branded content needs the Commercial Music Library (TikTok-only), and sources conflict on whether the Content Posting API can attach CML tracks ([bundle.social](https://bundle.social/tiktok-commercial-music-library)). Licensed music APIs are contract-gated: Epidemic Sound's Partner API needs a signed agreement and Soundstripe's is negotiated, with no public prices ([Epidemic](https://www.epidemicsound.com/business/developers/), [Soundstripe](https://soundstripe.com/api)). Suno and Udio are in flux after label settlements, with downloads restricted and later disputes reported inconsistently ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/11/26/warner-music-group-settles-copyright-case-with-suno-for-licensed-ai-music), [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/09/suno-universal-music-lawsuit-settlement-impasse/)). A fingerprint check on upload (AudD about $2-5 per 1,000 file requests, first 300 free, with figures that may be dated; ACRCloud pricing is login-gated) costs well under a cent and supports a repeat-infringer policy, but a match does not prove the user lacks rights ([AudD](https://audd.io/)). Standard terms of service (rights warranty, indemnity, DMCA agent, short retention and auto-deletion of uploads and renders) are the researchers' suggestions, not sourced.

## Recommended architecture, per-video costs and pricing

**Where the blueprint changes.** (1) Beat tracking: librosa becomes Beat This (small or final model) with an octave clamp, run on a CPU worker. (2) Structure and mood: add a DSP energy segmenter snapped to downbeats and an LLM keyword step; skip essentia and MERT models unless a commercial license is bought. (3) Clip selection: query Pexels (portrait) and Pixabay per job, rank with SigLIP 2, reject internal cuts with a shot detector, normalize to a short-lived mezzanine cache keyed by clip ID, and do not build a persistent mirror. (4) Rendering: MoviePy becomes FFmpeg subprocesses with frame-index cuts and untouched audio mux, behind a Dramatiq/Celery-style queue with SSE progress; GPU only if profiling justifies it. (5) Audio: default to a "no-audio export plus cut list" mode, or private-only audio renders, with DMCA agent, repeat-infringer policy, short retention and upload fingerprinting. (6) Paid media: add AI-generated hero shots as a metered premium tier, not the base.

**Cost per video** (all inference from the sourced unit prices; benchmark before relying):

|---|---|---|
| Free/stock-only | Beat This CPU (~$0.001-0.003), LLM (~$0.01), render ($0.004-0.01 on Modal L4 or CPU), stock free, storage small | about $0.02-0.06 |
| Vecteezy-assisted | Adds ~$0.10 per clip at $50/month for 500 downloads ([Plainly](https://www.plainlyvideos.com/blog/stock-video-api)); embedding rights unverified | roughly $0.5-1 for 5-10 clips |
| Hybrid AI | ~8 s of AI hero shots plus stock | about $0.40-0.80 over free tier |

AI price sources are aggregators and conflict in places: Veo tiers come from an August 2026 roundup ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-video-model-pricing)), Runway credits at $0.01 each ([Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/runway-api-pricing-per-second-for-every-video-model)), fal ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/pricing)). OpenAI's Sora 2 API reportedly sunset on 2026-09-24 while another source dated 31 October still lists it, so treat it as unavailable; one secondary claim that Veo 3.1 preview models shut down on 22 October 2026 is unconfirmed. Resellers often charge 2-3x official rates. Latency per clip, vertical 9:16 support, fixed 5/8/10-second clip lengths versus beat-aligned cuts, and per-vendor commercial terms were not verified. Premium stock libraries (Storyblocks, Shutterstock, Pond5) reportedly require roughly $24,000-$40,000 per year commitments, per Plainly's aggregator research only ([Plainly](https://www.plainlyvideos.com/blog/stock-video-api)), so they wait until paying volume exists. Spotify's audio-features and analysis endpoints were restricted from 27 November 2024 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/birrings/spotifys-audiofeatures-api-died-in-2024-heres-what-i-built-to-replace-it-3dn3)) and Cyanite is quote-only, so self-hosted analysis is the only durable choice.

**Competitor anchors.** CapCut Pro is about $9.99-19.99 per month depending on region, now with monthly AI credit allowances ([BigVU](https://www.bigvu.tv/blog/capcut-pricing-2026-free-vs-pro-included-alternatives)); Kapwing Pro $16-24 ([SocialRails](https://socialrails.com/blog/kapwing-pricing)); Canva Pro about $10-15; InVideo AI Plus $20-25 and Max $48-60 ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ai-video-tool-pricing-answered)); Pictory and VEED figures conflict between sources; Higgsfield runs about $15-19 for 200-270 credits up to $99-129 for 3,000 ([Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog/higgsfield-pricing-explained-2026-unlimited-credits-and-real-monthly-costs)). Beat sync itself is a standard feature in CapCut and mobile editors, so the differentiator must be fully automatic generation from a song. Free-to-paid conversion benchmarks run about 2-5% for consumer apps and 8% median for SaaS ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)), secondary summaries.

**Suggested pricing (researchers' proposal, not validated).** Free: stock-only, watermarked, capped renders, 720p (cost about $0.02-0.06 per video; at a 3-5% conversion each payer subsidizes roughly 20-30 free users, so the cap matters). Pro at about $9.99-14.99 per month: stock-only HD/1080p, no watermark, fixed monthly render allowance, plus a small credit pool for hybrid AI shots; an 8-stock plus 2-hybrid month costs about $2 or less in COGS. Metered credit packs for all-AI videos, priced at least 2-3x cost to absorb retries. Do not offer the stock-library tier until licensing is clear.

## Risks and MVP roadmap

The ranked risks are: (1) uploaded-song copyright and platform muting or claims, with unclear safe-harbor fit, needing a lawyer before launch; (2) commercial-use license traps in analysis models (madmom, essentia, MERT, possibly SongFormer weights, allin1 dependencies) and ambiguous training-data terms even for Beat This; (3) stock API terms that can revoke keys for caching or "replication", plus weak vertical supply and repeated identical results ([Pexels FAQ](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/900005538826-Why-do-I-get-the-same-content-from-multiple-requests)); (4) volatile AI-video pricing and API sunsets; (5) accuracy failures on slow, classical or irregular songs (octave errors, non-periodic beats); (6) cloud price shifts such as Hetzner's. Mitigations are the user-edit step for tempo octave, an energy-based fallback grid, and a "report issue" path.

An MVP roadmap, ordered by risk reduction: first, a two-week spike that runs Beat This on 50 varied songs (include slow and non-Western tracks) and measures x86 runtime, and a 50-keyword Pexels/Pixabay portrait availability test, while counsel reviews the music and stock terms and drafts terms of service. Second, build the pipeline end to end for stock-only, 30-second vertical output: Beat This, rule-based cut table, SigLIP 2 ranking, FFmpeg frame-index render, audio muted or private-only, with a browser preview and a proxy render. Third, ship a free watermarked tier with DMCA agent registration, repeat-infringer policy, upload fingerprinting and short retention. Fourth, add Pro billing, the energy-based structure step, and optional SongFormer or allin1 after license clearance. Fifth, add AI hero-shot credits (start with the cheapest vendor that supports 9:16) and consider Vecteezy or a licensed music partner once volume justifies a contract.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the blueprint's centre of gravity: the hard part is not beat detection or rendering, which are nearly free and well understood once licensing-clean models and direct FFmpeg are chosen, but the rights around the song and the clips. That makes "mute-export plus beat map" and private-only rendering more than a fallback; they are the design that lets a free tier exist at roughly two to six cents per video without the company reproducing commercial music at scale.

Much of what the team would otherwise assume remains unmeasured: Beat This on x86, NVENC versus CPU for vertical stock, vertical clip supply, Pexels/Pixabay storage rules, AI-clip latency and 9:16 support, and every commercial-use question for the structure and mood models. Run the cheap spike first; those results, plus a lawyer's view on audio, decide whether the paid hybrid-AI tier is a profitable upsell or just a cost line.
